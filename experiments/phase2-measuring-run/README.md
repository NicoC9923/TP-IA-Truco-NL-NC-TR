# Phase 2 measuring run

The [pre-registered rule](RULE.txt) was amended before the run to use the auxiliary head off. With user approval, segment A ran with 12 workers, mixed targets, panel v0, seed 42001, check base 43000, 240 matches per generation, temperature 0.1 and a 480-second cap. It ended at the cap after 485.3 seconds, 12,000 matches, 50 generations, 49 checks and three promotions (generations 2, 3 and 27); the final snapshot is generation 50, not the generation 27 champion. The runner, benchmark and analysis each exited successfully ([done](done.json)).

Whole-segment throughput was **24.73 matches/s** and **327.69 learner samples/s**, including checks. Training-only throughput estimated from checked-generation pre-check elapsed was **130.24 matches/s**. The independent head-off benchmark measured **11.79 seconds per check** and **10.13 ms per learner update**. Applying the registered formula gives **57,120 matches per generation**, estimated at 450.4 seconds (7.5 minutes), with 2.6% check overhead. These are pilot estimates: the timing comes from an eight-minute, undertrained network and early short matches; matches may lengthen and training throughput may fall, invalidating the estimated duration and overhead.

The fixed replay selected **T = 0.05**: the median top legal-action probability at T=0.1 was 0.336 among 34 learner decisions, below 0.40. The descriptive tactical-mistake rate was 0/34. The held-out profile comparison selected **prior strength 5**, with mean log losses 0.591, 0.623 and 0.651 for strengths 5, 10 and 20, respectively, on 15 scored opportunities. The auxiliary head remains off by the prior user decision. Both replay sample sizes are very small, come from one undertrained snapshot, and are weak evidence for stable settings.

## Replay investigation

The exact nine preregistered replays were reproduced without changing the decisions or selecting new seeds; the recorded metrics matched exactly. The temperature sample consists of the learner's decisions against itself and four phase 1 opponents: those five matches had 10, 1, 1, 1 and 2 hands and 24, 1, 2, 2 and 5 learner decisions respectively. In each of the three one-hand matches an accepted falta envido ended the match immediately. The remaining four styled matches had 7, 5, 7 and 2 hands; they add profile evidence but do not enter the temperature statistic. The collector's recorded learner counts matched engine decisions for each match, so no replay collection bug was found.

The prior analysis excludes self, resets each opponent profile, and scores a rate only from its second opportunity onward. The four phase 1 matches provided no scored opportunities; the four styled matches contributed 8, 2, 5 and 0, respectively. No revealed-hand bluff opportunities were recorded. Thus the 15 observations reflect both short matches and the held-out rule, rather than a missing collection pass; their scarcity limits the comparison.
