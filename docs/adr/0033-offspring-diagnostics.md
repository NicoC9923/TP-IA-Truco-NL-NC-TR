# Offspring and behavioral diversity diagnostics

Status: accepted, pre-registered before diagnostics.

On the frozen generation 70 population, uniformly sample 32 parent pairs with replacement, reserving four population members as opponents because scores are absent from the checkpoint. Measure parents and five offspring variants against the same fixed opponents with 32 mirrored pairs per network, and compare paired changes across parent pairs using normal 95% confidence intervals. Use a separate bank of 2,000 legal decision observations generated by population matches and compare probability distributions with total variation and argmax agreement, including panel-v1 as a scale. Distinct odd seed namespaces isolate each diagnostic. The bank encodes each observation with a fresh default opponent profile; this fixes the context across all networks.

Pre-registered rules:

1. Crossover damage: the 95% CI of paired crossover-plus-mutation minus sigma-0.05 mutation-only win-rate changes is entirely below zero; proposal 4 is indicated.
2. Mutation too weak: sigma-0.05 mean argmax agreement exceeds 0.98, its win-rate change CI includes zero, and its across-pair change standard deviation is below 0.044 * sqrt(64/N), the ADR 0032 pure-noise spread scaled to N pairs. Try a larger sigma.
3. Mutation too destructive: sigma-0.05 mean change CI is entirely below zero, fewer than 40% beat their parent, and sigma-0.025 mean change is at least as high. Try a smaller sigma.
4. Low behavioral diversity: population mean pairwise total variation is less than one third of panel mean pairwise total variation; proposal 6 is indicated.

Report all fired rules; choose one change using the largest standardized threshold exceedance: rule 1 uses the magnitude of the paired difference divided by its CI half-width, rule 2 the argmax excess over 0.98 divided by 0.02, rule 3 the magnitude of the mean divided by its CI half-width, and rule 4 one minus the ratio of population to one-third panel distance. If no rule fires, state: no operator diagnosis; consider inputs (proposal 7). These comparisons describe one population and a finite fixed opponent set, not a causal training outcome.
