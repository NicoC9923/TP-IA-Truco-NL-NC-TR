# Observable inference and profile conventions

The exact filter represents original three-card hands and removes a hand only for certain evidence. The observation exposes accepted envido declarations in mano order: mano declares, and pie either declares a higher value or says son buenas; played cards remain visible in the table. At hand end, an engine decision is marked revealed only when that player's three cards have actually been played. The profile uses only such revealed hands for its bluff statistic, counting a hand once if any call in it was a bluff. Fold and envido rates are per observed hand, while truco acceptance is per opportunity; each starts at 0.5 with a 20-observation prior.

A truco call multiplies each possible hand's weight by `b + (1-b)*(0.1 + strength/24.7)`, where b is the measured bluff rate and strength is the existing rules module's hand power. This positive likelihood is a simple calibrated-by-profile heuristic, not a learned likelihood; it preserves every compatible hand and becomes neutral at b=1. The summaries describe remaining cards: bravas are the four highest individual cards, strength is the existing weighted hand power, and next-trick win probability averages uniformly over the opponent's remaining cards against a specified card, with ties controlled by the caller. Uniform choice is an explicit baseline because the opponent's card-selection policy is unknown.

A learned behavioral model and a best-card assumption were rejected: the former requires training data and the latter implies an unjustified policy. In imperfect-information games, filtering impossible states before applying a likelihood and a prior is a standard Bayesian decomposition; the simple likelihood here leaves room for future calibration without changing the exact filter.
