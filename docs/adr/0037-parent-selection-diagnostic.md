# Parent selection diagnostic

Status: accepted; preregistered before either real checkpoint is evaluated.

For each frozen seed 1 checkpoint at generations 10 and 70, evaluate its saved population, hall and reigning champion with the trainer's league and checkpoint configuration at its configured pair count (16). The saved population is the next generation, so schedule style rotation uses checkpoint generation plus one. A separate 64-pair league gives an independent measurement on a common schedule; each schedule uses its own random stream and odd deal seeds in the reserved namespace `2*(10^23 + stream*1000000 + pair)+1`, distinct from the existing training, validation, panel, ranking, offspring, behavior and input-audit namespaces. The checkpoint's encoder must be v1. These are frozen evaluations, not training.

Draw 200 tournaments with three independent uniform population indices with replacement, exactly as in training; the first maximum of the first-pass scores wins. Compare its independent score to the mean of the other two candidate slots. Report the mean difference, the fraction of tournaments in which the winner is best on the independent score, and the same quantities for a randomly selected candidate slot. Split best credit equally among tied slots, including repeated individuals, so chance is one third. Report the score difference divided by the sample standard deviation of independent population scores; if spread is zero, the ratio is undefined.

For a paired 95% percentile interval, resample population individuals with replacement 2,000 times, preserving each individual's paired first-pass and independent scores, and redraw 200 tournaments from each resampled population. This accounts for repeated individuals and tournament sampling, but conditions on the particular measured schedules and does not quantify schedule or training-run variation. The fixed random seed and all scores and schedules are recorded to make results reproducible.

Selection is **reliable** only when the lower bound for winner minus losers is strictly above zero in both populations. Otherwise the verdict is **no demonstrated selection advantage**, accompanied by the observed estimates and uncertainty; this does not establish that the genetic algorithm is broken. These two populations from one training seed and one independent schedule per population cannot establish generality across seeds, generations or schedules.
