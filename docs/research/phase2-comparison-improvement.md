# Phase 2 comparison and improvement: evidence and next experiments

Evidence reviewed 2026-11-10. This note explains the finished four-mode tournament and proposes future work; no new matches or training were run. “Algoritmos genéticos” denotes the phase 1 method; existing artifact and code keys remain unchanged.

## What won, and what that result means

Fixed Adaptive Utility won this particular comparison: its equal-weight mean against the other three modes was **75.300%**, versus **61.233%** for the shipped reinforcement learning (RL) agent, **34.383%** for Reactive, and **29.083%** for the phase 1 Algoritmos genéticos agent. These are arithmetic means over three different opponents, not rates against a shared opponent pool or confidence intervals for the ranking. Adaptive Utility beat Reactive 83.15%, Algoritmos genéticos 79.70%, and RL 63.05%. RL beat Reactive 68.15% and Algoritmos genéticos 78.60%, but scored 36.95% against Adaptive Utility. Each pairing had 1,000 mirrored pairs, half at each target, and its own descriptive interval; see [results and intervals](../../experiments/four-mode-round-robin/README.md), [frozen rule](../../experiments/four-mode-round-robin/RULE.txt), and [raw records](../../experiments/four-mode-round-robin/results.json).

The immediate answer to “why did it win?” is that this fixed heuristic made decisions that produced more match wins against all three particular opponents under this protocol. It is a strong hand-written strategy, but the aggregate scores do not identify which decisions caused its advantage. The wrapper receives its own hand and observable state, including public table, calls and scores; it does not receive the opponent's private hand ([reference interface](../../proyecto/engine_policies.py)). Despite its name, its wrapper never invokes online adjustment: it was fixed for these matches ([ADR 0059](../adr/0059-adaptive-utility-stays-non-adaptive-in-run2.md)). Fresh policies were used per game. Results are descriptive for these modes, settings and streams; their intervals are not simultaneous and do not prove universal dominance.

The compared neural agents differ in architecture, training setup, budget and selection; phase 2 run 1 also trained against selected phase 1 networks. This is not a controlled comparison isolating RL versus Algoritmos genéticos. Run 1 did **not** train against Reactive or Adaptive Utility, although they were used for evaluation. The unshipped run 2 did train against both and included them on its selection panel; their scores then concern familiar strategies ([run 1](../../experiments/phase2-run1/README.md), [ADR 0043](../adr/0043-phase2-league.md), [ADR 0056](../adr/0056-phase2-run2-references-train-with-the-model.md), [ADR 0057](../adr/0057-phase2-run2-fixed-six-member-panel.md), [ADR 0060](../adr/0060-phase2-run2-claims-and-held-out-networks.md)).

## Why more training can fail to improve the graph

A generation is a point in one evolving training run; an independent run starts afresh with a different seed. Additional generations may improve estimates and expose the learner to more situations, but updates can also trade performance between opponents, amplify noisy choices or follow a changing opponent distribution. More independent seeds measure how much outcomes vary and whether a result repeats; they do not continue the same learner. Neither more generations nor more runs guarantees monotonic improvement or victory over a strong heuristic. Extra compute is useful only if the objective, opponents and exploration cover the relevant weaknesses; this is a hypothesis to test for this project, not a diagnosis established by the graph. Henderson et al., [Deep Reinforcement Learning That Matters](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11694) (2018), document sensitivity to seeds and experimental settings and motivate reporting variability.

Locally, each recorded learner decision receives the final match outcome from its seat, +1 or −1; Adam minimizes squared error for the chosen action, rather than directly optimizing an individual bid or hand score ([trainer](../../proyecto/phase2_trainer.py), especially lines 191–195 and 590–610; [ADR 0040](../adr/0040-phase2-network-and-inputs.md)). The policy samples legal actions using softmax at fixed temperature 0.05 in the published run ([policy](../../proyecto/phase2_torch.py), lines 109–133; [ADR 0041](../adr/0041-phase2-action-selection.md)). This can leave an undervalued action underexplored or introduce errors through stochastic choices, but neither effect has been measured as the cause of this loss. The original [DouZero paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/zha21a.html) (Zha et al., 2021) demonstrates deep Monte Carlo with self-play and parallel actors in a different imperfect-information card game; it is a useful design reference, not evidence that its choices would improve this Truco agent.

The shipped model is run 1 generation 28, not its last generation. Run 1 improved on the two references from generation 4 to 10 by a paired +17.6 ± 2.7 percentage points, while generation 28 versus 10 was −1.1 ± 2.5: no measurable late improvement on those references. Nineteen subsequent checks failed to promote before the cap; generation 48 was interrupted before its check. Promotion controls select gains on a panel, with a direct screen, not guaranteed improvement against every opponent ([run 1 record](../../experiments/phase2-run1/README.md), [ADR 0047](../adr/0047-phase2-champion-selection.md)).

Run 2 generation 38 is an unshipped candidate, **not proven worse**. Its frozen one-time acceptance direct score against generation 28 was 51.85%, 95% interval [49.975%, 53.725%]; the direct lower bound did not exceed 50%. Its equal-weight held-out difference was +3.075 percentage points [1.553, 4.597], positive for those two held-out networks. The prescribed decision was inconclusive and the existing champion remains shipped ([run 2 results](../../experiments/phase2-run2/README.md), [acceptance output](../../experiments/phase2-run2/acceptance/output.txt), [ADR 0058](../adr/0058-phase2-run2-one-time-acceptance-test.md)). The held-out set is small and limited, and neither result establishes broad general strength.

## What remains uncertain

A prior descriptive diagnosis reports points lost against Adaptive Utility around accepted envidos and truco folds/refusals ([run 1 README](../../experiments/phase2-run1/README.md)). That motivates a targeted breakdown, but does not establish that any specific betting decision was mistaken, or that a particular training change would fix it. A narrow run 1 league, the match-level reward and exploration settings are plausible contributing mechanisms, not measured causal explanations.

The network already receives up to 24 ordered public events from the current hand plus a profile summarizing earlier hands: its flat input is 1,628 → 256 → 128 → 12, with no LSTM ([encoder](../../proyecto/phase2_network.py), [ADR 0040](../adr/0040-phase2-network-and-inputs.md)). Therefore simply “add history” is not a supported recommendation. The post-training probe's hidden-card accuracy was 11.208% versus 10.708% for a random-trunk control, while combination log loss was 6.955 versus 6.944; these descriptive intervals do not establish superiority, nor does the probe show that representation caused match losses ([probe results](../../experiments/phase2-probe/RESULTS.txt)).

## Prioritized next work

1. **Measure existing records first, without new games.** Break down the current paired outcomes by target and seat, with counts and uncertainty at the mirror-pair level where appropriate. The round-robin raw records contain target, seeds and winning seats, not bid-by-bid actions; inspect other existing logs before attempting call or decision breakdowns, and explicitly mark unavailable fields. Use the run 1 betting diagnosis as a descriptive lead, not a causal conclusion. This is cheap and can identify where a later investigation is worthwhile.
2. **Replicate with independent training seeds and report uncertainty before doubling a 12-hour run.** Pre-register a feasible small number of seeds, equal budgets and fixed evaluation, and report each run plus seed-level variability; avoid treating thousands of games from one trained model as independent training replicates. Short throughput and diagnostic checks should precede any costly run, and use at most 12 CPU workers. This distinguishes training instability from the effect of longer training; seed replication itself does not guarantee a stronger model.
3. **Only if diagnostics justify it, pre-register one explicit training comparison.** For example, test a revised exploration temperature or opponent mix against its unchanged control at equal wall time, using multiple seeds and a newly specified validation panel and untouched holdout with fresh schedules. Select the change based on the observed weakness, define outcomes and stopping rules in advance, and test generalization to opponents outside training and selection. Avoid changing architecture, reward and league simultaneously. Existing run 2 acceptance is frozen: no retry, relabeling, threshold change or checkpoint shopping; a future study needs its own protocol and independent evaluation ([run 2 rule](../../experiments/phase2-run2/RULE.txt)).
